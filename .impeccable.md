## Design Context

### Users
Richard (the sole user) — a full-stack developer upskilling in AI/LLMs. Checks this at a desk to answer "what's the state of the art in LLMs right now?" without opening a dozen leaderboard tabs. Wants signal, not noise: scan the numbers, spot what changed, move on.

### Brand Personality
Precise, confident, engineered. Reads like an instrument, not a marketing page. Three words: **instrument, dense, unimpressed.** It states facts and gets out of the way.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Data terminal / instrument** — information-first, high data density done legibly, tabular precision. Dark theme (desk use, dev tool). Numbers are the hero; type and color exist to make them scannable and comparable.

- Tabular/lining numerals with aligned columns are core (the table IS the product). Monospace is acceptable *for numerals only* — NOT as a blanket "technical vibe" font for prose/labels.
- One restrained accent, used rarely (rank movement, the active sort, a live signal). 60/30/10 weight.
- Motion is minimal and functional (state changes, a single considered load reveal) — no decorative micro-interactions.

### Anti-references (explicitly avoid)
The **generic AI dashboard** look: cyan-on-dark, purple/blue gradient text or fills, glowing/glassmorphic cards, identical rounded-corner card grids, hero-metric tiles (big number + tiny label + gradient). No pure #000/#fff. No sparklines as decoration.

### Design Principles
1. **The table is the instrument.** Optimize for scanning and column comparison — tabular numerals, tight rhythm, clear column hierarchy, quiet zebra/hover, no heavy gridlines.
2. **Numbers first, chrome last.** Type weight/size and one rare accent carry hierarchy; borders and boxes are a last resort, not the default.
3. **Honest about freshness.** Provenance ("data as of") and staleness are first-class, never buried.
4. **Confident restraint.** Dark, precise, calm. If it could pass for a generic AI dashboard, it's wrong.
